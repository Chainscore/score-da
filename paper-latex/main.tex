\begin{abstract}
Data availability (DA) bandwidth bounds the throughput of rollups and sharded blockchains.
This paper presents a benchmarking study of six production DA protocols (Polkadot ELVES, Ethereum EIP-4844/PeerDAS, Celestia, Espresso Tiramisu, NEAR, and Avail) using 92 days of mainnet data (November 2025 to February 2026).
We introduce a taxonomy that classifies DA architectures into three paradigms: data availability sampling (DAS), verifiable information dispersal (VID), and validator-set recovery.
Under a unified methodology we measure throughput (MiB/s), cost (\$/MiB), utilization, and block dynamics across all six protocols.
Key findings are: (i)~window-mean utilization of the protocol maximum ranges from 0.18\% (NEAR) to 35\% (Ethereum); Ethereum ran at $\sim$60\% of its maximum ($\sim$90\% of its fee-market target) until the BPO forks raised capacity, and the other five protocols stayed below 3\%; (ii)~median DA cost spans nearly seven orders of magnitude, from \$3.1$\times$10$^{-9}$/MiB (Espresso, a fixed placeholder fee) to \$0.020/MiB (Celestia), although the six cost measures differ in what they include; (iii)~no protocol slashes for pure data withholding, so enforcement is indirect in every design; and (iv)~no protocol guarantees permanent availability: default retention ranges from $\sim$25~hours (Polkadot) to $\sim$18~days (Ethereum), while Avail and Espresso nodes retain data unless operators enable pruning.
Ethereum's Fusaka upgrade and two blob-parameter-only forks fall inside the window, allowing before--after comparisons of fee-market behaviour.
We release all collection tooling, datasets, and analysis code under open licenses.
\end{abstract}
