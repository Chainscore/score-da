% SECTION VIII: CONCLUSION
% ================================================================

We measured six production DA protocols (Polkadot ELVES, Ethereum EIP-4844/PeerDAS, Celestia, Espresso Tiramisu, NEAR and Avail) with one methodology, using 92~days of mainnet data covering about 18.8~million blocks.

\subsection{Summary of Findings}

Window-mean utilization ranged from 0.18\% (NEAR) to 35\% (Ethereum). Ethereum ran at $\sim$60\% of its maximum until the BPO forks raised capacity; the other five protocols stayed below 3\%.
Throughput comparisons across these networks therefore measure adoption rather than capacity.

Median DA cost spans nearly seven orders of magnitude, from $\sim$\$3$\times$10$^{-9}$/MiB (Espresso, whose 1~wei/byte fee is a Mainnet~0 placeholder) to \$0.020/MiB (Celestia); the six measures differ in which fee components they include.
On Ethereum, the blob fee left the 1-wei floor at Fusaka, coinciding with the EIP-7918 reserve price and before the BPO forks added capacity.
Annualization reorders the ranking: NEAR's spot cost (\$0.010/MiB) is close to Avail's and Ethereum's, but its annualized cost (\$2.4/MiB/yr) is the highest because of its $\sim$1.5-day retention.

No protocol slashes for pure data withholding.
Polkadot gates candidate inclusion on availability bitfields, Celestia uses bad-encoding fraud proofs, Ethereum uses peer scoring, Espresso relies on committee reputation, NEAR relies on its chunk-inclusion rule, and Avail slashes only for equivocation.
Attributing withholding, an omission fault, to a specific actor is still unsolved.

No protocol guarantees permanent availability.
Default retention ranges from 25~hours (Polkadot) to $\sim$18~days (Ethereum); Avail and Espresso retain data unless operators enable pruning; and among the four with a default horizon, only Ethereum's exceeds a 7-day optimistic-rollup challenge period with margin.

\subsection{Future Work}

\emph{Longitudinal extension}: repeating these measurements as utilization rises would show supply-constrained behaviour that the current data cannot.
\emph{Stress testing}: controlled synthetic load would measure protocol limits directly, which observational data cannot do.
\emph{Withholding enforcement}: incentive-compatible enforcement for omission faults needs formal analysis.
\emph{Cross-layer analysis}: measuring end-to-end rollup performance (confirmation time, cost, finality) for each DA backend would show how the choice affects rollups.
\emph{PeerDAS at scale}: PeerDAS activated during our window; its effect on node bandwidth and on further BPO increases remains to be measured.
