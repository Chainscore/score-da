\subsection{Byzantine Fault Tolerance}
\label{sec:security-bft}

All six protocols inherit the classical BFT bound of $f < n/3$, where $f$ is the number of Byzantine-faulty nodes and $n$ is the total committee or validator set size.
How the threshold is applied differs between protocols.

\paragraph{Polkadot}
Polkadot's relay chain requires availability bitfields from $\geq 2/3$ of validators before a parachain candidate is included; GRANDPA finality is then gated on approval checking.
With approximately 600~active validators, the system tolerates up to $\sim$200 Byzantine faults.
ELVES adds approval checking: randomly selected validators re-execute parachain blocks post-inclusion, and a single invalid execution triggers a dispute that can slash the responsible validators~\cite{Jeff_Burdges_ELVES}.
The probability of an invalid block escaping detection decreases exponentially with the number of approval checkers.

\paragraph{Ethereum}
Ethereum's Casper FFG requires a $2/3$ supermajority of validators (by stake weight) to finalize blocks.
The active set had approximately 1~million validators during the window.
PeerDAS assigns column custody per node ID, with additional custody groups per 32~ETH of attached validator balance; availability of every column depends on custody being spread across many honest nodes~\cite{EIP-7594}.

\paragraph{Celestia}
Celestia runs CometBFT (Tendermint) consensus, which requires $\geq 2/3$ of validators (by stake weight) to commit each block.
The validator set is capped at 100 in the current configuration.
Light-client sampling adds a second check: if validators finalize a block whose data is unavailable, sampling detects the withholding with high probability~\cite{AlBassam_FraudDA}, provided enough light clients take part.

\paragraph{Espresso}
Espresso's HotShot consensus operates with 100~nodes across 20~operators in the Mainnet~0 configuration.
The DA certificate requires $\geq 2/3$ of committee signatures, tolerating up to $\sim$33 Byzantine nodes.
However, the small operator count (20) means that the effective trust assumption is closer to $f < 7$ at the operator level~\cite{Espresso:Hotshot_and_Triamisu}.

\paragraph{NEAR}
NEAR's Nightshade design applies the BFT threshold per shard~\cite{NEAR_Nightshade_Scaling_Blockchain}: chunks are produced and validated by the validators assigned to each shard, so the assumption is that fewer than one third of each shard's assigned stake is Byzantine.
With 9~shards and approximately 400~validators, the size of each shard's assignment depends on the stake distribution.

\paragraph{Avail}
Avail's BABE/GRANDPA consensus requires $\geq 2/3$ honest validators for both block production and finality.
DAS light clients provide independent verification, analogous to Celestia's model.
The VectorX bridge to Ethereum adds a further trust dependency: bridge liveness depends on the prover infrastructure, and the approximately 2-hour bridge delay creates a window during which Avail-native finality and Ethereum-bridged attestations may diverge~\cite{Avail_The_DA_Blockchain}.

\subsection{Data Withholding Enforcement}
\label{sec:security-withholding}

Section~\ref{subsec:verification} showed that no protocol slashes for data withholding; here we compare how each one detects it.

Detection is sampling-based for Celestia, Avail and Ethereum (missing responses to random samples), attestation-based for Polkadot and NEAR (a missing bitfield or chunk endorsement is visible on-chain, but only validator-level withholding is detected), and certificate-based for Espresso (a missing DA certificate blocks finalization).

\paragraph{Enforcement gap}
In every protocol, detecting that data is unavailable does not identify who is responsible.
A validator that fails to serve a chunk may be offline, network-partitioned, or maliciously withholding, and the protocol cannot distinguish these cases.
A plausible reason no protocol slashes for withholding is that slashing on such evidence would also punish honest nodes that have network problems.

\subsection{Retention and Persistence}
\label{sec:security-retention}

No protocol guarantees permanent availability (Table~\ref{tab:feature_comparison}). Four have a default retention horizon on non-archival nodes: Polkadot's availability store keeps finalized chunks for 25~hours, NEAR garbage-collects after 5~epochs ($\sim$1.5~days), Celestia light nodes sample within 7~days (CIP-036), and Ethereum serves blobs for 4{,}096~epochs ($\sim$18~days). Avail and Espresso define no network-wide expiry: Avail nodes keep block data by default and Espresso nodes choose their own pruning policy, so availability beyond the latest blocks rests on operators rather than the protocol.
An optimistic rollup needs the DA layer to keep data for at least its challenge period, typically 7~days.
Polkadot (25~h) and NEAR ($\sim$1.5~d) keep it for less and Celestia's 7-day sampling window leaves no margin, so rollups on these layers must archive data themselves or shorten their challenge periods; on Avail and Espresso it depends on node configuration.
Bridges that verify DA attestations must also complete within the retention window; Avail's VectorX attestations reach Ethereum about 2~hours after the block.
After expiry, data survives only if full nodes, indexers or rollup operators archive it voluntarily.
